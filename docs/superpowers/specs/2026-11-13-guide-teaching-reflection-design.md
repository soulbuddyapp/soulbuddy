# Guide and teaching reflection design

## Purpose and scope

Replace the teaching completion form with an encouraging, private reflection guided by a recurring illustrated companion. Establish a reusable identity and name preference, but introduce the Guide only in daily teaching and its name setting in this first release. Later uses in prayer, Scripture, goals and remembrance require their own content and interaction design. The Guide is a fictional companion inspired by a guardian angel; it is not identified as the person's actual angel and never claims divine authority or speaks for God.

## Visual identity

The Guide is a non-human mascot, not a human angel character. The exploratory direction is a small, rounded seed-like creature with a simple expressive face, a smooth rounded head without an antenna or sprout, leaf-like wings, small softly shaped limbs and a grounded stance. Use a hand-painted editorial style, warm paper texture, muted sage, terracotta and honey. Its silhouette must remain recognizable at small sizes; expressions should be attentive and kind, without judgment. A consistent luminous golden halo signifies the Guide’s connection to God across expressions. Convey heavenly strength through radiant light, a grounded posture, a confident presence and expressive eyes; do not use muscular anatomy as a symbol of power. Avoid clerical costume, realistic sacred figures and gamified rewards. The expression library includes listening, relatable frustration, inspired energy with a warm flame motif, and confident protectiveness with a shield of light and an assured stance. Frustration expresses solidarity with difficulty, never disapproval of the person; courage is symbolic and never promises supernatural protection. Avoid weapons or aggression. Do not infer a person’s emotion from their private words; select expressions by the explicit context and let reflection remain calm. The revised mascot concept is exploratory, not a production sprite sheet: create consistent, individually exported welcome, listening, encouragement, frustrated, inspired and protective illustrations, with transparent backgrounds, before shipping. No text is baked into illustrations. Keep text legible in normal Flutter widgets, and provide a text-only fallback for missing assets and reduced motion. Illustrations are decorative and excluded from accessibility semantics when adjacent text already conveys their meaning.

## Introduction and name

After a person explicitly confirms a teaching is finished, introduce the Guide once before the first reflection. Show “Meet your Guide”, a single randomly selected invented name from the fixed, editorially reviewed list Arel, Elio, Neri and Oria, and a field to change it. Names are fictional, not presented as names of actual angels. The user may accept the suggestion or enter a name; trim whitespace, require 1–40 Unicode characters, and report a clear error for an empty or overlong name. Persist the chosen name locally only after the user confirms; an interrupted introduction is offered again with a fresh suggestion. The name is editable in Settings. Avoid global onboarding interruptions and never block existing users from continuing a teaching because a preference cannot load or save: show a retry and an option to continue without a name.

## Reflection flow

After a finished confirmation, show the Guide and one short, day-specific invitation grounded in the day's focus, not a claim about what a particular video said or what the person felt. Offer an optional second, practical question. Each answer accepts typed or spoken text. Both are optional; completion still requires the existing explicit Finished/Complete action. Do not treat opening a video or dismissing the sheet as completion. Preserve entered text while moving within the flow and when speech is unavailable. A scrollable, keyboard-aware layout must work at large text sizes and on small screens.

For day 1, invite: “When did you last feel truly seen?” and “Who could you help feel seen today?” For day 2: “What do you reach for when life feels heavy?” and “What could give you a moment of life today?” For day 3: “What small choice has been troubling your conscience?” and “What is one safer choice you can make next time?” Avoid presuming guilt, hardship, or a particular emotional response. Day-specific copy needs editorial and theological review against the actual focus and selected teaching options. For days without approved new copy, retain the existing day reflection and action text as a fallback, so all 70 days remain usable; never present fabricated video-specific insight.

## Privacy and data

The current repository stores a completion, including reflection and action, in secure storage under `daily-formation.progress.v2`. Preserve that format and older completion records. Map the two answers to the existing `reflection` and `action` fields, keeping optional empty values absent in serialized JSON. This first release adds no sharing, AI processing, analytics payloads or network transmission of names or answers. The existing formation activity event may continue sending only its video ID and activity type. Clearly label the reflection “Private reflection”; private means device-local in this flow, not an implied cross-device backup. Name preference is also device-local. No migration, deletion or upload of historical responses is required.

A completion is committed before closing the flow. If storage fails, retain both inputs, show “Could not save. Try again.”, and allow retry; do not advance the day or record a completed event until save succeeds. Dismissal does not persist drafts or advance. This does not add an externally visible share action; any future sharing requires an explicit separate choice and review.

## Verification and accessibility

Test initial random selection and persistence, custom naming and editing, failed reads/writes, and no forced introduction for returning Guide users. Test day-specific approved prompts and fallback, both answers optional, existing completion compatibility, speech and keyboard access, sheet dismissal, save failure/retry, and event ordering. Capture payloads to verify the activity request contains no name or response text. Verify long text, large text scaling, small screens and screen-reader order on Android. Verify the Guide's presence never obscures content and no animation is required to use the flow.
